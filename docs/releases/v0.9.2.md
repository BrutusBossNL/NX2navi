## Install or update

- **Already have 0.6.0 or newer:** start the launcher; it offers 0.9.2, updates itself and keeps the game it compiled
  and the shaders it prepared. Only what changed is compiled again.
- **Already have 0.5.0:** the same, and the game's code 0.5.0 compiled is kept as it was compiled (for any x86-64
  processor); *Rebuild the Game* in the launcher's home compiles it again for yours.
- **New install:** download **`NX2navi-Setup-0.9.2.exe`** below, put it next to your `.nup` dump of the game and start
  it. Windows SmartScreen warns on the first start because the setup is not signed: choose *More info* and *Run
  anyway*. The launcher checks your game files, builds the game (10 to 25 minutes, once), prepares every shader and
  starts it.

**Requirements:** Windows 10 or 11; an x86-64 CPU with SSE4.2; 16 GB of RAM; about 30 GB free for the game and 1.5 GB
for the launcher; a GPU with Vulkan 1.3 and `VK_EXT_external_memory_host` (current NVIDIA and AMD cards). A GPU without
`VK_EXT_mesh_shader` runs the game's mesh shaders as compute shaders (new in this release, see below).

## New

- **Grass and foliage on graphics cards without mesh shaders** (AMD before RDNA 2, such as the RX 400 and 500 series
  and Vega; NVIDIA before the GTX 16 and RTX 20 series). The game draws its grass, moss and other foliage with mesh
  shaders. On a card without them these now run as compute shaders, and what they make is drawn as ordinary
  triangles. It is on by default on such a card and does nothing on any other; `mesh_emulation = 0` in nx2navi.ini
  goes back to what 0.9.1 did there. Written and run on a Radeon RX 580 by Tobim6.
- **Video memory kept within what the card has free.** Textures and buffers are allocated within the budget the
  driver reports for each memory heap, with 512 MB kept free (`vram_reserve_mb`), and a texture that does not fit the
  first heap goes to the next one with room. A large batch of texture uploads is handed to the GPU early instead of
  growing until a submit runs out of memory. By Tobim6.
- **A settings tab for older and slower cards**, *Lama patches*, by Tobim6: how many released textures are kept, the
  video memory reserve, the upload batch size, mesh shader emulation, foliage detail (`foliage = full`, `reduced` or
  `off`), and three settings that stay off unless you choose them: preparing frames on a thread of their own
  (`replay_async`), keeping memory pools the game refills every frame in system memory (`mirror_hot`), and compiling
  the game's code at -O2 at the next *Rebuild the Game* (`guest_opt = 2`, a build several times longer).
- **Link's own motion for DLSS, FSR 3.1 and frame generation** (`selective_motion = 1` in nx2navi.ini, off by
  default): Link and the equipment attached to him are drawn a second time with last frame's pose, so the upscaler
  gets their real motion in fast turns, attacks and jumps instead of an estimate. It has not been played by us yet. By
  automata-rtx (pull request #699).
- **A Switch used as a controller over USB** (it shows up as "Nyx Joy-Con") is taken as a gamepad. Any other joystick
  the game cannot use is named in the log once, with its ids. By Tobim6.

## Fixed

- **FSR and DLSS showed a blocky, shaking picture with the HUD hidden.** The upscaler's picture was put where the
  game draws its HUD. With the HUD off the game skips that step, and the frame's last step then drew the game's own
  plainly scaled scene over it: the screen showed the scene at its render size (a third of the picture at Ultra
  Performance) in visible blocks, shaking by up to one render pixel from frame to frame, with a white line along the
  bottom or right edge in about half the frames, while the upscaler's work was thrown away. Native AA was affected
  as well as the scaling modes. The upscaler now runs where the game's own upscaler does, HUD or not. On a save with
  the HUD off at FSR 4 Ultra Performance: fine detail is back, the picture moves 0.02 pixels between screenshots of a
  still view instead of jumping by 3, and no screenshot has the white line (half had it).
- **A crash after switching FSR modes.** One FSR context served every mode, with another render size at each switch,
  and the first frame after a switch from Quality to Native AA could end the game inside AMD's driver. Each render size
  now gets a context of its own, as AMD's own sample makes them, and the one before goes a few frames later (a mode
  switch takes about 50 ms more); with dynamic resolution on, one context made for changing sizes takes every step.
- **Bright patches along the bottom edge over water, and a light line at the edge, with FSR or DLSS.** To let the
  upscaler build a smoother picture the scene is drawn moved by a fraction of a pixel, and AMD's driver then leaves
  the last row or column undrawn. Those are now filled from their neighbours as soon as each step of the scene is
  drawn, before the water, the bloom or the upscaler read them.
- **A new start for the upscaler at every frame of a quick camera swing.** A fast swing of the camera around Link (11
  to 18 degrees a frame) was taken for a cut to a new view. Only a jump of the camera or a turn of 30 degrees or more
  in one frame counts as a cut now.
- **Objects drawn grey for a frame while you move.** While you move the game brings in sharper versions of about 32
  textures every four or five frames, each in a texture slot that showed the coarser one until then. A frame made 16
  of them and drew the others as their plain stand-in until the next round: lamp posts, the windows of a house front
  and patches of ground went flat grey for one frame. A slot that shows a texture, and one the game has just
  registered, now gets its new texture in the same frame. In 600-frame recordings of walks through a village, 1,525
  such slots went blank before and none after.
- **Textures arriving late in a busy frame.** The time a frame may spend making textures also counted the checks of
  textures already made, so in a busy area the limit was reached before anything new was made. Only the making counts
  now.
- **One frame nearly black, with its colours exchanged.** The game reads its scene through a second description of
  the same memory in another format. That texture was dropped every 600 frames as overwritten although the game still
  read it, and the frame that dropped it made it again from the scene's raw bits: in a player's recording one frame at
  a sixth of its brightness. A texture that is still read is kept now.
- **Teal splotches in the fog of Kokiri Forest.** The game puts its per-frame render targets over each other in
  memory, and the fog read its volume after the bloom target had been drawn over it. A texture read in that state gets
  memory of its own from then on. By Tobim6.
- **The game hanging with the draw replay on several cores.** Two replay threads could wait for each other at the
  end of a render pass, each holding the lock the other needed. By leonardochappuis (pull request #697).
- **Lines drawn one pixel wide whatever width the game set.** The width is passed on now where the card supports wide
  lines.
- **Stripes of self-shadow on slopes with `shadow_scale` at 2 to 4.** A shadow map drawn larger got the game's slope
  depth bias unchanged, which is too little for its smaller texels. The bias grows with the map now; at
  `shadow_scale = 1`, the default, nothing changes. By FIocker (pull request #705); not run in the game by us yet.
- **A generated frame blended from two unrelated pictures at a camera cut, with FSR 3.1 frame generation.** Frame
  generation is told of the cut and starts from the new picture. By FIocker (pull request #703).
- **Frame generation on cards whose graphics queue family has a single queue** presents through another family, and
  the picture without the HUD is also used where the upscaler's output is smaller than the presented picture. By
  Tobim6.
- **A build that was cut short while it generated the game's sources** was taken for finished at the next try. The
  launcher runs the generation again now. By DeepDaddyTTV (pull request #360).
- **Linux: each submission is handed to the driver's own submit thread** on Mesa drivers, so the draw replay does not
  wait for it. By DerHalbGrieche (pull request #701).

## Changed

- **Draws and uploads wait less for the GPU.** The pages of game memory a submission needs are copied while the draw
  replay records it, so the game's wait after a frame's first submission ends once the replay has handed it over instead
  of when the GPU has run it; a submission whose results the game reads back is still waited for. The texture pool has
  8 descriptor sets in turn instead of 4. By Tobim6.
- **The recording thread is used on AMD cards without mesh shaders** (RX 400 and 500, Vega, RX 5000). It stays off on
  the newer AMD cards, where it lost the device. On a Radeon RX 580 the replay's part of the game's wait after a
  frame's first submission went from 12 ms to 6.9. By Tobim6.
- **The flight recorder** (`flight = 1`, F8) keeps its pictures at 640x360 instead of 320x180 and notes texture slots
  that lose their picture, what an upload read and slot registrations. A captured frame also lists the draws that were
  rejected while it was prepared (by DeepDaddyTTV, pull request #501). New for reports: a key that dumps the next
  frame (`dump_key`), the GPU's time per render pass (`pass_times = 1`) and, in a crash report, what the crashing
  thread was building (by Tobim6).

## Changed

- **FSR 3.1 is sharper at Balanced, Performance and Ultra Performance.** It was soft there, most of all while the
  camera moved: turning the camera at a steady rate, FSR 3.1 Performance kept 60 % of Native AA's fine detail and Ultra
  Performance 50 %, where FSR 4 keeps 89 and 81 %. Below a render scale of three quarters, FSR 3.1's own sharpening now
  goes towards its maximum and its picture gets a second, lighter sharpening pass before the HUD is drawn; both follow
  the Sharpness slider, and 0 turns them off. Performance now keeps 85 % and Ultra Performance 71 %, for about 0.2 ms
  a frame. FSR 4 and Native AA are unchanged.
- **Debug menu: the free camera has no limits.** It flies where it looks and goes through walls, objects and the
  ground; it used to keep its height and climb whenever anything in the lower part of the view came within three
  metres, a character or a rock it was flown towards included. *Ground avoidance* is now a switch in the free camera's
  section, off unless you turn it on: with it the camera stays a metre above fields and gentle hills, and objects and
  walls never push it.

## Known issues

- At FSR's and DLSS's smaller modes, Link and other characters look a little coarser while they move: the upscaler
  gets only the camera's motion for them, not their own. `selective_motion = 1` in nx2navi.ini gives it
  Link's own motion; it is new and off by default (see New).
- Ultra Performance gains little frame rate over Quality with FSR 4. Most of a frame is the replay of the game's draws
  on the CPU, which does not shrink with the render size, and FSR 4 itself takes 3.1 ms of the GPU's time at Ultra
  Performance and 3.9 ms at Native AA on a Radeon RX 7900 XT (FSR 3.1: 0.5 ms). Measured standing in Hyrule Field at
  3416x1440: FSR 4 Native AA 52 fps, Quality 53, Ultra Performance 55; FSR 3.1 Native AA 55, Ultra Performance 66;
  without an upscaler 70.
- The changes for older cards (mesh shaders as compute, the video memory budgets, the shorter waits for the GPU) were
  run by their author on a Radeon RX 580. Please report how they behave on other cards.
- FSR 4, its frame generation and the AMD-only fixes were not run by us on the cards they are for before this
  release.
- The first visit to an area after an install can still pause for a moment while its textures are decoded.
- Integrated AMD GPUs can fail at the title screen (their host memory heap is too small for the game's memory).
- Nothing in this release was run on Linux by us.
